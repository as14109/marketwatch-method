# Pyramid 50/30/20 strategy

**Definition:** An **aggressive** [[position-scaling]] method — adding to a winner as it proves itself
— that builds to a **full** position in three tranches of the *ideal* size: **50% → 30% → 20%**.
Use only on a strong stock in a strong market.

## How it works
- **Buy 1:** 50% of ideal position.
- **Buy 2:** +30% of ideal, only after the stock shows real continued strength (not a blip).
- **Buy 3:** +20% of ideal → full position.
- **Worked example (ideal 1,000 sh):** 500 → +300 → +200 = 1,000.
- Even here, **sell some into strength** as it gets extended — see [[selling-into-strength]].

## When to add (technical triggers)
- After a **breakout from a sound base**.
- On a **pullback to a key moving average (10-day / 21-day) that then bounces**.
- When the stock **forms a new base and breaks out again**.
- Set buy points **in advance**; require ironclad discipline; if it doesn't hit the point, don't force
  it ("there's always another bus coming"). These triggers are classic [[can-slim]] / IBD technique.

## Why add to winners (the rationale)
- "Trends tend to continue more often than they reverse" — press the advantage on a proven winner.
- **Risk is capped early:** if it tanks after Buy 1 you've only risked half the intended size.
- **Psychology:** confidence builds with each tranche because the stock keeps proving itself —
  "test drive before you put a ring on it."

## When NOT to use
- Faint-of-heart / "squirrelly" markets, or weak stocks. Not for every name or every condition —
  reserve for **best ideas in the strongest markets**. In choppy regimes use [[smart-add-on-strategy]].

## Notable example
- Presenter [[pat-walker]] describes pyramiding into [[amzn]] from its late-90s bookstore days,
  building a large stake with an average cost well below the eventual market price.
