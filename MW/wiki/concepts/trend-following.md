# Trend Following (Michael Covel & the classic trend-followers)

**Definition:** The systematic approach of riding sustained price trends — entering on strength, cutting
losers fast, and letting winners run for as long as the trend persists. Synthesized from Michael Covel's
*Trend Following* and the broader canon (Darvas, Livermore) for our strategy use; not a text reproduction.

## The pillars
1. **Cut losses short.** A predefined stop is non-negotiable; small controlled losses are the cost of doing
   business. No averaging down.
2. **Let winners run.** The edge comes from a few large trends paying for many small losses. Do **not** cap
   winners with fixed profit targets — exit on trend reversal, not on a price you picked in advance.
3. **Price is the signal.** Follow the trend of price, not opinions, forecasts, or fundamentals.
4. **Position sizing / risk parity.** Size by volatility and a fixed % of equity at risk; survive the
   drawdowns so you're present for the big trend.
5. **Emotional discipline.** Follow the system through drawdowns; the hardest part is doing nothing while a
   winner pulls back within its trend.

## Positive expectancy shape
Trend systems typically have a **low win rate (~30–40%)** but a **large win/loss ratio** — the opposite of
"be right often." This is exactly the profile of our book of record (24–34% win rate, big runners carry it).

## How it maps to our system
- **Direct validation of exit v3.** Our move from a +12% profit target (capping winners) to a **50-SMA
  trend-hold** ([[key-list-trade-rules]]) is textbook trend-following. The [[drift-review-2026-07-25]]
  finding — "the fixed target destroyed the edge" — is the classic mistake Covel warns against.
- **Cut losses short** = our **−12% disaster stop**.
- **Let winners run, but protect the trend** = the 50-SMA exit + the **+30%→+15% profit-lock**
  ([[profit-protection-2026-07-25]]), a one-way ratchet that banks nothing early but guards a big gain.
- **Low win rate is expected**, not a bug — see [[expectancy]].
- **Caveat the canon shares with us:** trend-following bleeds in choppy/rangebound regimes. Our
  **regime gate** ([[regime]]) is the defense — reduce entries when there's no trend to follow.
