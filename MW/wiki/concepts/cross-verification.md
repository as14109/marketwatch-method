# Cross-verification

**Definition:** [[alan-farley]]'s principle that the **highest-probability, lowest-risk trades occur where
multiple *independent* support/resistance methods converge** on the same price ("confluence"). One signal
is noise; several lining up is an edge.

## Sources of S/R that can converge
- Moving averages (the **MA Ribbon** — e.g. our 9/21/50/200 stack)
- Prior swing highs/lows and consolidation edges
- Fibonacci retracements/extensions (e.g. the 38–62 zone)
- Bollinger Bands
- Round numbers / psychological levels
- Trendlines and gaps

## Why it matters
- Converging levels give a **precise entry and a tight, logical stop** (just beyond the cluster) → better
  reward:risk and smaller losses ([[expectancy]]). Isolated levels don't.

## How it connects to our process
- Argues we should **rank Key List names by confluence**: a pivot where the 9/21/50/200 MAs *and* a prior
  high cluster is higher-odds than an isolated breakout. Ties to [[trend-template]] (MA stack) and the
  [[seven-bells]] (each setup is strongest at a cross-verified level).
