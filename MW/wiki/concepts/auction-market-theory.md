# Auction-market theory

**Definition:** [[peter-steidlmayer]]'s framing that a market is a continuous **two-way auction whose
purpose is to facilitate trade**. Price moves to advertise opportunity and to find the level that
facilitates the most volume — a **fair price** / value — then rotates around it until new information
moves value. [[market-profile]] is the tool that visualizes this.

## Core principles
- **Purpose = facilitate trade.** Price probes higher to find sellers and lower to find buyers; it settles
  where the most business gets done (value).
- **"Price cannot simultaneously serve two masters."** A move that's good for buyers isn't for sellers —
  this tension *is* the opportunity.
- **At / above / below value.** The trader's first job is to locate price relative to **[[value-area]]**;
  away-from-value prices are advertisements that tend to be answered (responsive trade) unless backed by
  new conviction (initiating trade / range extension).
- **Excess marks turning points** — fast, unanswered moves leave reference levels.

## Why it matters / how it connects
- Provides the **value/mean-reversion counterpoint** to momentum's trend-following: momentum buys
  *strength* (initiating, above value); auction theory also recognizes **responsive** value-buying below
  value. Knowing *where price is relative to value* reconciles the two — see [[selling-into-strength]]
  (trimming into above-value strength is consistent with both).
- Related: [[market-profile]], [[value-area]]; contrast: [[can-slim]], [[pyramid-50-30-20-strategy]] (trend/initiating).
