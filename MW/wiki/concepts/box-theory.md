# Box Theory (Nicolas Darvas)

**Definition:** Nicolas Darvas's method (*How I Made $2,000,000 in the Stock Market*) of framing a rising
stock's consolidations as **"boxes"** — trading ranges bounded by a recent high and low — and buying only
when price breaks **above** a box on volume, then trailing the stop up box-by-box. Synthesized for our use;
not a text reproduction.

## The method
1. **Find leaders in strong uptrends** — only stocks making new highs with rising volume.
2. **Define the box.** A stock consolidates in a range; the top and bottom of that range are the box.
3. **Buy the breakout** above the top of the box (a fresh box forming higher = confirmation).
4. **Trail the stop** just below the current box's floor; as price stacks higher boxes, the stop ratchets
   up — never down.
5. **Ignore the noise.** Darvas traded on price/volume action alone, deliberately tuning out tips, news,
   and opinions ("techno-fundamentalist": strong stock **and** breakout).

## How it maps to our system
- **Entry = box breakout.** Our Key List **trigger** is a box-top breakout level; we buy only as price
  breaches it ([[key-list-trade-rules]]), no chasing gap-ups — Darvas likewise bought *at* the breakout.
- **Box-by-box trailing** is the ancestor of a **volatility/structure trailing stop**. Our 50-SMA trend-hold
  is a smoothed version; a stricter Darvas trail would exit on a break of the last consolidation low.
- **Only new-high leaders** = the strength filter in [[trend-template]] / [[can-slim]].
- **Refinement idea:** a **box/swing-low trailing stop** (exit on a close below the prior consolidation low)
  is a candidate alternative to the 50-SMA for the fastest-moving names — testable against 2024/2025/2026.
