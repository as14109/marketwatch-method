# Market Timing — "Don't fight the tape" (Martin Zweig)

**Definition:** Martin Zweig's (*Winning on Wall Street*) discipline of gauging the **overall market's**
odds before taking risk, combining **monetary**, **momentum/tape**, and **sentiment/breadth** signals — and
never fighting the prevailing trend. Synthesized for our regime work; not a text reproduction.

## The signals
- **"Don't fight the tape."** Trade with the market's direction; when breadth and momentum are negative,
  raise cash regardless of how good individual setups look.
- **"Don't fight the Fed."** Monetary conditions (rate trend, liquidity) set the backdrop; easing is a
  tailwind, tightening a headwind.
- **Momentum / breadth thrusts.** Powerful up-moves in advance/decline breadth and volume signal the start
  of durable advances; deteriorating breadth warns of tops.
- **Sentiment as a contrarian gauge.** Extreme optimism often marks tops; extreme pessimism, bottoms.
- **Earnings growth** for stock selection; strict **stop-loss discipline** (Zweig sold quickly on
  breakdowns).

## How it maps to our system
- Our **8-point regime score** ([[regime]]) is a Zweig-style composite: SPY/QQQ/IWM trend (tape),
  RSP/SPY breadth, VIX level+trend (sentiment/volatility), distribution-day count. The **gate** ("only
  press in a confirmed uptrend") is literally "don't fight the tape." ([[portfolio-plan]] §1.)
- **Gap / enrichment idea:** we don't yet include a **monetary** input ("don't fight the Fed") or a formal
  **breadth-thrust** trigger. Adding a rates/liquidity tilt or an advance-decline thrust to the regime
  score is a candidate refinement — but keep it simple; the current score already works as the drawdown
  defense ([[ytd-review-2026-07-25]] showed the gate is what capped the July loss).
- **Stop discipline** aligns with our −12% disaster stop and trend exit.
