# Position scaling

**Definition:** Building (or reducing) a stock position in increments rather than entering or exiting
all at once, so that size tracks conviction and confirmation instead of a single timing decision.

## Why it matters
- Caps the damage from a bad initial entry — you only have partial size if the trade fails early.
- Lets winners grow while keeping losers small; ties capital committed to evidence accumulated.
- Manages **psychology**: smaller starting risk → lower stress → clearer decisions ([[pat-walker]]).

## "Trade small before you trade big" (Minervini)
Start with a **pilot position** (5–10%) and **add only as the trade proves itself**; let *banked
profits* finance bigger risk, "pyramiding your way to bigger positions instead of working out of a
hole." Increase exposure on success, cut back (even to cash) when out of sync. See [[eight-keys]],
[[expectancy]]. [[mark-ritchie-ii]] runs the same playbook (50 bps → scale up only on results).
